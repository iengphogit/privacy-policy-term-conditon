Educational use & safety

Updated 7 October 2026

Made for learning

SketchPORT supports STEM and Arduino learning for students aged 13 and older, teachers and adult learners, including classroom exercises, personal learning and non-production research. It is not directed to children under 13. Its editor, code examples, AI explanations, calculators and component illustrations are learning aids.

SketchPORT is not designed, tested or certified for company production projects, commercial product design, industrial control or safety-critical work. Do not rely on it to design, approve, certify or operate a product or installation where an error could injure someone, damage property or interrupt an essential service. Classroom and research prototypes still require supervision and independent checks.

Check before connecting hardware

Software results do not prove that a physical circuit is safe. Real components have tolerances, temperature limits, power ratings and failure modes that examples and models may omit. Check the manufacturer's datasheet, pinout, polarity, wiring, voltage, current and power limits. Use appropriate current limiting, protection and qualified supervision. Disconnect power before changing wiring.

Keep learning experiments to suitable low-energy, current-limited supplies under supervision. Low voltage alone does not guarantee safety. Do not use this app as guidance for mains electricity, high-voltage or high-current equipment, hazardous battery systems, medical devices, vehicles, life-support or emergency systems. Seek qualified engineering support for those uses.

Unexpected operation and safe testing

Uploading, resetting, opening a serial connection, reconnecting USB or restoring power may restart firmware or change output states. Motors, relays, heaters and other connected loads can activate unexpectedly, including when an upload fails or the connection is lost. Do not assume an error message, disconnected phone or stopped app leaves equipment safe.

Before connecting, uploading or testing, disconnect hazardous loads and make the circuit safe using the equipment manufacturer's procedures. Keep people clear of moving parts, hot components and exposed conductors. Test code first with harmless indicators or otherwise isolated outputs, using a controlled setup and qualified supervision. Check startup, reset and connection-loss behavior before connecting any load that could cause harm. Do not leave experiments unattended. SketchPORT is not a protective device or an emergency-stop system.

Cancelling AI, closing the app or unplugging the phone does not stop firmware already running on a powered board, undo an upload or necessarily turn off outputs. An externally powered board can continue operating. Use appropriate physical power isolation or equipment-specific stop controls; do not rely on a software stop command to make hardware safe.

If something goes wrong

If equipment behaves unexpectedly, stop the experiment and keep others away. Disconnect power only when it is safe to do so, following the equipment's safety procedures; do not touch hot, energized or moving parts. Obtain qualified help before reconnecting or resuming. For injury, fire or immediate danger, use local emergency procedures rather than app support.

Report suspected app faults to iengpho@gmail.com with the app version, board model, a description of the issue and the steps leading to it. Share only diagnostic information you are authorized to disclose. Never send API keys, passwords or confidential project files. A support report does not stop connected hardware and is not an emergency response service.

AI and research limits

AI can produce incorrect code, explanations, calculations or references. Review suggestions and independently test code and circuits before use. A successful compilation or upload is not a safety assessment. Component pictures are illustrative, not manufacturing drawings. Future simulation results will also be approximations; simulation is unavailable in MVP1.

Validate research results with appropriate methods and instruments. The app does not provide professional engineering advice, certification or a guarantee of suitability for a particular experiment.

Your responsibilities and rights

Follow your school or laboratory rules and local law. Obtain appropriate supervision, keep backups and review what firmware will do before uploading it. Uploading replaces the board's existing program and can change attached hardware behavior.
